Receiver location

Where the antenna (and the screen) are. Distances, directions and "look up" angles are measured from here. Click the map to move the pin.

Aircraft data

Planes are marked on their cards: Your antenna or adsb.lol only, and the online-only ones are hollow on the map. Handy for seeing how much of the sky your antenna catches.

Which way the screen faces

The arrow on the display points from the viewer to the aircraft. For that to work it needs to know which compass direction a person is facing while looking at the screen. On the display, “up” means “beyond the screen”, so a screen hung on a south-facing wall means the viewer faces north.

Calibrate with a plane you can see

Stand where you'd look at the screen. When you spot a plane roughly straight ahead (beyond the screen), tap it below. Low, close planes work best.

Cycling through aircraft

When something is close and low (probably the plane you're looking at) the display sticks to it instead of cycling. Avoid large values if you live next to an airport.

Photos & routes

FlightAware

FlightAware's AeroAPI fills in routes the free sources don't know, with the real take-off time and its landing estimate. It's paid per query, so Look Up only asks about planes in the air near you that nothing else could place, and stops for the month at the budget below. Needs FLIGHTAWARE_API_KEY set on the server; never used with the simulator.

Daily traffic

The daily traffic page can show where flights from your local airport go, and where its arrivals come from. A flight counts when it's seen low near the airport.

Air traffic control radio

Listens to tower and ground radio, works out which plane each call is to or from, and shows what's going on in plain words on that plane's card, playing the calls as it comes up. Needs the whisper speech-to-text service running (see the README); plain-English summaries by Claude need ANTHROPIC_API_KEY set, otherwise a simpler built-in description is used.

Special aircraft

Police, air ambulance, military and government aircraft get a coloured badge. Many are recognised from the aircraft database; add your own here by registration (C-FSPS), callsign or hex code. End with * to match the start of a callsign (STAR*). Tick Alert to show a notice on the display whenever it's in range.
